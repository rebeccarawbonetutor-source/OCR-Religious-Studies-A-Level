Developments in Christian Thought

Augustine's Teaching on Human Nature →

Genesis 3 and the Fall, relationships pre- and post-Fall; Original Sin and its effects on the will and human societies; God's grace overcoming sin.

Death and the Afterlife →

Heaven, hell and purgatory as places, spiritual states or symbols; election — limited, unlimited and universalist; the parable of the sheep and the goats (Matthew 25:31–46).

Knowledge of God's Existence →

Natural knowledge through innate sense of the divine and order of creation (Calvin, Romans 1:18–21); revealed knowledge through faith, grace, Christ, Church and Bible (Barth).

The Person of Jesus Christ →

Jesus' authority as Son of God (miracles, resurrection), teacher of wisdom and liberator; his relationship with God and divinity.

Christian Moral Principles →

Bible as sole authority; Bible, Church and reason combined; love (agape) governing Christian practices; whether Christian ethics are distinctive.

Christian Moral Action →

Bonhoeffer: Church and State, obedience and civil disobedience, Confessing Church and Finkenwalde, costly grace and suffering; relevance today.

Religious Pluralism and Theology →

Exclusivism, inclusivism ("anonymous" Christians) and pluralism; whether Christ as "truth" allows other means of salvation and whether a loving God denies salvation.

Religious Pluralism and Society →

Contemporary multi-faith societies and migration; Christian responses to inter-faith dialogue (Catholic Redemptoris Missio 55–57, Church of England Sharing the Gospel of Salvation) and scriptural reasoning.

Gender and Society →

Christian teaching on roles of men and women in family and society (Ephesians 5:22–33, Mulieris Dignitatem 18–19); responses to secular views on gender, motherhood and family.

Gender and Theology →

Feminist reinterpretation of God: Rosemary Radford Ruether and Mary Daly on patriarchy, maleness of Christ and whether Christianity can be reformed or abandoned.

The Challenge of Secularism →

Rise of secularism and secularisation; God as illusion and wish fulfilment (Freud, Dawkins); secular humanist views on public life and responses from believers.

Liberation Theology and Marx →

Marx's alienation and exploitation; structural sin, preferential option for the poor and orthopraxis over orthodoxy; debates on engaging with Marxism.
