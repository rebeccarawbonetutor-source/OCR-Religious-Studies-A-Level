Philosophy of Religion - Soul, mind and body

1. Plato & Aristotle (Forms, Recollection & Hylomorphism)

Core Definitions

Key Definitions:

  • Dualism: The view that there are two different types of existence: mental and physical.
  • Monism: The view that there is one kind of existence.
  • Materialism: The view that existence is strictly physical substance.
  • Substance Dualism: Descartes' version arguing that mental (characterised by thinking) and physical (characterised by extension) are unbreakable fundamental substances.
Plato's Dualism & Analogies

Plato's Dualism:

  • Prison Metaphor: The body acts as a prison trapping the soul in a world of appearances, while souls originate from the unchanging World of Forms.
  • Degrees of Reality: Minds and abstract forms represent a higher degree of reality than the physical body.
  • The Charioteer Analogy: Compares the soul to a charioteer (reason) driving a noble horse (spirit/honour) and an unruly horse (appetite/desire). Harmony leads to truth; dominance leads to ignorance.
Plato’s Argument from Recollection & Cycle of Opposites

Anamnesis & Argument:

  • A Priori Knowledge: We possess knowledge of perfect concepts (justice, beauty, perfect circles) never fully experienced in the physical world.
  • The Meno: Socrates demonstrated that an uneducated slave boy could solve geometry, proving innate geometric concepts.
  • Cycle of Opposites: Argues souls cycle between life and death.
Criticism
  • David Hume: Argues beauty and justice are subjective and culture-dependent rather than objective perfect concepts.
  • Abstract Negation: Hume claims we form perfection by conceiving 'not imperfect' without needing an innate soul.
  • Evolutionary Alternative: Evolution could have programmed humans with basic mathematical intuition and moral senses for survival.
  • Cycle Flaws: Critics note life and death may not be objective opposites, and the expanding universe contradicts eternal balancing.
Aristotle’s Materialist Opposition & Hylomorphism

Empirical Rejection: Aristotle rejected Plato's World of Forms as lacking empirical validity and explanatory power for change, functioning like an early Ockham's razor.

Hylomorphism & Soul
  • Formal Cause: Form is an inseparable essence defining what a thing is.
  • Stamp in Wax Analogy: The soul is the formal cause of the body (giving humans the essence of reason), just like a stamp imprint gives character to wax without being a separate entity.
Criticism
  • Francis Bacon: Established the modern scientific method, rejecting formal causation as a metaphysical concept beyond empirical study.
  • Scientific Reduction: Modern science shows properties like snow's whiteness reduce to particle interactions (efficient/material causation), suggesting rational thought reduces to physical brain structures rather than Aristotelian formal causes.

2. Descartes & Substance Dualism

Descartes’ Framework & Cogito Ergo Sum

Rationalist Foundation:

  • Substance Attributes: Mental substance is characterised by thinking (non-extended); physical substance by extension (occupying space/coordinates).
  • Cogito Ergo Sum: Indubitable knowledge begins with 'I think therefore I am', as doubting is a form of thinking.
  • Foundational Doubt: Descartes used dreams and the evil demon hypothesis to separate the clear intuition of the thinking mind from the doubtful physical body.
Descartes' Indivisibility Argument

Indivisibility Argument: Physical substance is divisible because it is extended in space; the mind is indivisible and non-extended. By Leibniz's Law (identical things must share identical properties), mind and body cannot be identical.

Criticism
  • Scholastic Objection: Argued the mind can be divided into distinct modes like perception, memory, and emotions.
  • Split-Brain Evidence: Modern medical evidence from split-brain patients (whose corpus callosum is severed) shows consciousness can exhibit divisions, such as hands acting independently or opposing each other.
Descartes' Conceivability Argument

Conceivability Argument: If we can clearly and distinctly conceive of the mind separate from the body (e.g., as an immaterial ghost), they are possibly and thus actually separate.

Criticism
  • Antoine Arnauld: Used a geometric example (conceiving a right-angled triangle without knowing Pythagoras' theorem) to object to Descartes, which later inspired the formal structure of the Masked Man Fallacy.
  • The Masked Man Fallacy: The fallacious inference that because one can conceive of X without Y, X and Y must be distinct (e.g., conceiving of a bank robber as *not* your father, even though the masked man actually *is* your father).
  • Limits of Introspection: Critics argue Descartes wrongly assumes infallible self-knowledge, whereas cognitive psychology shows much mental processing is unconscious.
The Interaction Problem

The Problem: If mind and body are distinct substances, how can they interact causally (e.g., mental desire moving an arm)?

Criticism
  • Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia: Argued that physical interaction requires mechanical contact (pushing), which a non-physical substance cannot provide.
  • Pineal Gland Critique: Philosophers note that Descartes' suggestion of the pineal gland merely assigns a physical location rather than explaining the mechanism of interaction.
  • Causal Closure: Modern physicists argue substance dualism violates the principle of causal closure derived from the second law of thermodynamics, which dictates that energy cannot enter or leave the physical universe from an external non-physical source.

3. Ryle, Behaviourism & Modern Materialism

Gilbert Ryle’s Category Mistake & Behaviourism

The "Ghost in the Machine": Ryle argued dualism commits a category mistake (like asking to see the "university" separate from its buildings). The mind is not a non-physical "thing".

Philosophical Behaviourism
Mental terms refer to behavioural dispositions rather than inner entities (analogous to glass brittleness being a disposition to shatter, not a physical object itself).
Criticism
  • Qualia & Subjectivity: Critics argue philosophical behaviourism fails because it ignores the subjective, qualitative feel of conscious awareness ("what it feels like" to have an experience).
  • Reductionism: Reduces rich inner mental life to mere outward tendencies.
Dawkins, Materialism & Reductive Materialism

Materialist Monism: Dawkins argues there is no empirical evidence for a soul (Soul One); humans are evolved physical beings composed of DNA and biological matter. "Soul Two" is used purely as a metaphor for emotional/intellectual depth.

Reductive Materialism (Smart)
Applies Occam's Razor: mental states (pain) are ontologically identical to brain states (C-fibre stimulation), supported by correlations from drugs, brain damage, and neuroimaging.
Criticism
  • Subjective Omission: Critics argue reductive materialism overlooks subjective experience.
  • David Chalmers: Points out that identifying mental states with brain states leaves out the qualitative dimension of consciousness.
The Hard Problem & Property Dualism

David Chalmers' Objections: Neuroscience solves the "easy problems" (mapping brain functions to mental tasks), but the "hard problem"—why physical processes give rise to subjective consciousness—remains unexplained.

Materialist Defence
  • Patricia and Paul Churchland: Argue that the brain's complexity means reductive explanations are currently inconceivable.
  • Methodological Preference: Extending physical science is methodologically superior to positing unverified non-physical entities, comparing eliminative materialism's success to historical dismissals of vital life forces.
